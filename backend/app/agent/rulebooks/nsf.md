# NSF rulebook

The rules the agent applies to a National Science Foundation (NSF) grant, beyond the checks code runs. The agent gets this whole file on every run. When an answer or a flag rests on a rule here, the answer names the rule's ID.

Each rule below was checked against its source on the date it shows. A rule goes in only after that check. Changing a rule is a change to this file.

**Sources and editions.**

- 2 CFR 200 (Title 2 of the Code of Federal Regulations, Part 200), the federal Uniform Guidance, as published on the Electronic Code of Federal Regulations (eCFR), current as of November 6, 2026.
- NSF's Grant General Conditions (GC-1), the edition dated July 31, 2026. GC-1 numbers its parts "Term and Condition No.".
- NSF's Prior Approval Matrix, dated October 1, 2024.
- The older conditions: the Research Terms and Conditions (RTC) overlay dated November 12, 2020, its Appendix A, Prior Approval Matrix (November 12, 2020), and NSF's RTC Agency Specific Requirements (ASR) dated May 20, 2024.
- NSF's Proposal and Award Policies and Procedures Guide (PAPPG), NSF 24-1, with Supplement 1 (NSF 26-200, for awards made on or after December 8, 2025) and Supplement 2 (NSF 26-202, for awards made on or after January 22, 2026, on public access and data sharing).

Where a rule says "the university", it means the recipient of the award.

**Which conditions govern an award.** GC-1 applies to NSF grants issued on or after October 1, 2024, and each GC-1 edition applies to new grants and to funding amendments dated on or after its own date. A grant issued before October 1, 2024 follows the conditions issued at the time of award: for a university, the RTC with NSF's ASR. The award notice, and each later amendment, states which conditions govern and their date.

**On an award under the older conditions.** Apply each rule as written. Where a rule has `- Older source:` lines, name both its GC-1 citation and its older citation; where it says `- Older status: unread`, the older conditions weren't found to say the same, so flag the charge instead of answering contradicts (`partly unread` means this for the part its note names). The older ASR citations are to its edition of May 20, 2024. Each passage a rule below cites from it was compared with the four earlier editions (November 12, 2020, October 4, 2021, May 13, 2022 and January 30, 2023), and the rule's `- Older editions:` line says what they say; on an award under one of them, name that edition's citation. For any other ASR passage, when the award cites an earlier edition, apply the rule from the May 2024 text and say in the reason that the award cites an earlier edition that hasn't been read, without flagging the charge for that alone. An award under an RTC edition before November 12, 2020 is handled the same way: apply the rule from the texts cited here and say in the reason that the award's own RTC and ASR editions haven't been read, without flagging the charge for that alone. Rules that rest only on 2 CFR 200 or the policy guide apply to both, but 2 CFR 200 is quoted here in its current edition, and an older award follows the edition in force when it was made.

**2 CFR 200 on older awards.** Every section of 2 CFR 200 cited here was compared with its text as in force on September 30, 2024, the day before the current edition took effect. Most were reworded but say the same in substance: 200.216, 200.308 (where today's (g)(1) and (g)(2) were (e)(1) and (e)(2)), 200.421, 200.423, 200.424, 200.429, 200.431, 200.434, 200.441, 200.442, 200.445, 200.449, 200.450, 200.451, 200.459, 200.463, 200.465, 200.469, 200.470, 200.471 and 200.475. Five passages differ in substance: 200.1 (participants), 200.413(c), 200.438, 200.454(c) and 200.461(b). The rules that cite them name the older wording on an `- Older source:` line with its quote. On an older award, name both citations. Where a rule has `- Older answer: from the governing wording`, answer from the wording that governs the award, not the current one. The reason names both citations, says which one governed and why (which conditions the award is under, from its confirmed terms), and says how the other differs. Where both wordings give the same answer, the reason still names both citations, and nothing more is needed. Where they give different answers and the award's conditions can't be told from its confirmed terms, answer unsure with both readings in the reason, without picking one. Where the older wording asks for prior approval, that approval is evidence like any other: on file and cited, or missing. For a rule that quotes an older 2 CFR wording without that line, name both citations and say in the reason how the older wording differs. Editions before November 12, 2020 weren't compared.

**How a rule's kind decides the answer.** Each rule sits under one of three kinds, and each rule repeats its kind on a `- Kind:` line. A not-allowed rule gives contradicts when the documents show that kind of cost, unless they show the exception the rule states. An allowed-under-a-condition rule gives supports when a document shows the condition is met, and contradicts when no document does. A judgment rule gives a flag, not contradicts, unless the rule itself names a comparison that gives contradicts.

## Kinds of cost allowed only under a condition

### NSF-AIRFARE-CLASS

Airfare above the basic least expensive unrestricted class is not allowed. A higher class is allowed only when the cheaper one would need circuitous routing, travel at unreasonable hours, excessively long travel, extra costs that offset the savings, or would not meet the traveler's medical needs, and the university justifies and documents that reason for the trip.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [2 CFR 200.475(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.475#p-200.475(e))
- Source: [NSF GC-1 (July 31, 2026), Term and Condition No. 10.a.2](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/gc1_7_31_2026.pdf)
- Older source: [NSF RTC Agency Specific Requirements (May 20, 2024), Article 14.a.2](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/nsf-research-terms-conditions-20240520-r.pdf)
- Older editions: the ASR editions of November 12, 2020, October 4, 2021, May 13, 2022 and January 30, 2023 say the same in Article 14.a.2.
- Quote: "the difference between economy airfare and a higher-class airfare is unallowable" (GC-1 No. 10.a.2)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-US-FLAG-CARRIER

Air travel to, from, between or within countries other than the U.S. paid with NSF funds must be on a U.S.-flag air carrier, or a code-share with one, when one is available, and the ticket must show the U.S. carrier's code and flight number. A foreign carrier is allowed only under GC-1's listed exceptions: an Open Skies agreement (such as the one with the European Union), involuntary rerouting by the U.S. carrier, the listed time and connection limits (for example, a U.S. carrier would add 24 hours or more to a trip to or from the U.S.), and short trips of three hours or less that a U.S. carrier would at least double; lower cost or convenience is not an exception.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [NSF GC-1 (July 31, 2026), Term and Condition No. 10.c and 10.d](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/gc1_7_31_2026.pdf)
- Older source: [NSF RTC Agency Specific Requirements (May 20, 2024), Article 14.c and 14.d](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/nsf-research-terms-conditions-20240520-r.pdf)
- Older editions: the ASR editions of November 12, 2020, October 4, 2021, May 13, 2022 and January 30, 2023 say the same in Articles 14.c and 14.d, apart from writing the Fly America Act citation as "49 USC".
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-MEALS-NOT-TRAVELING

Travel, meal and hotel costs of university employees who are not on travel status are not allowed. The policy guide also says no funds may be requested for meals or coffee breaks at internal meetings of the university or its parts, such as labs, departments and centers; that is proposal guidance and doesn't on its own make such a meal contradicts, unless the award notice restates it as a condition of the award, which makes it one of the grant's terms.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter X.C.3](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-10-allowability-of-costs)
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter II.D.2.f(xiii)(a) and (b)](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-2-proposal-preparation)
- Quote: "Travel, meal, and hotel expenses of recipient employees who are not on travel status are unallowable." (PAPPG X.C.3)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-PUBLICATION-FEES

Page charges, article processing charges and open access fees are allowed only when the publication reports work the federal award supported and the journal charges them on every item it publishes, federally funded or not. Publication costs not incurred during the period of performance may be charged during closeout, to the final budget period. Under the older 2 CFR wording, 200.461(b) names only page charges for professional journal publications, under the same two conditions; article processing charges and open access fees aren't named there.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [2 CFR 200.461(b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.461#p-200.461(b))
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter X.A.2.c, Post-End Date Costs](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-10-allowability-of-costs)
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Supplement 2 (NSF 26-202), item 1, revising Chapter XI.D.2.c](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/document/pappg24-1-supplement-2)
- Older source: [2 CFR 200.461(b) as in force from November 12, 2020 to September 30, 2024](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2024-09-30/title-2/part-200/section-200.461)
- Quote: "Special fees for the purpose of making published versions of record (VORs) publicly accessible are allowable costs but such payments are not required for deposits of AAMs in PAR." (PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 2)
- Quote: "Page charges for professional journal publications are allowable where:" (2 CFR 200.461(b), edition in force on September 30, 2024)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-POST-END-COMMITMENTS

NSF funds may not be spent after the award's end date, except to pay valid commitments made on or before the end date, such as a contractor's or subrecipient's services during the award period that were billed after it ended. Equipment or materials and supplies ordered after the end date generally may not be charged; the agent cites this rule when it disputes the date check with proof that the order or commitment was made by the end date.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter X.A.2.c, Post-End Date Costs](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-10-allowability-of-costs)
- Quote: "NSF funds may not be expended subsequent to the end date of the award, except to liquidate valid commitments that were made on or before the end date" (PAPPG X.A.2.c)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-CONSULTANTS

Consultant and professional service costs are allowed only for people who are not officers or employees of the university, when the cost is reasonable for the service and not contingent on recovering costs from NSF. The university must be able to justify that the rate of pay is reasonable.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [NSF GC-1 (July 31, 2026), Term and Condition No. 7](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/gc1_7_31_2026.pdf)
- Source: [2 CFR 200.459(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.459#p-200.459(a))
- Older status: unread (GC-1 No. 7's sentence on justifying the rate of pay wasn't found in the RTC overlay or the ASR of May 20, 2024; the 2 CFR 200.459 part applies to both)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-ADMIN-SALARIES

Administrative and clerical staff salaries are normally indirect costs. They may be charged directly only when all three hold: the services are integral to the award, the people can be specifically identified with the award, and the costs are not also recovered as indirect costs. On an award under the older conditions, they must also be explicitly included in the approved budget or have NSF's prior written approval: RTC Appendix A keeps that condition, 2 CFR 200.413(c)(3) in the edition then in force, for NSF. A budget line on the grant for those salaries, from the award notice, satisfies it. When they aren't in the approved budget, the agent adds NSF's prior approval to the charge's evidence checklist, naming this rule in the item's reason, so the charge stays in Missing evidence until an approval is linked; it doesn't answer contradicts or flag, because code's standing approval list comes from the current matrix.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [2 CFR 200.413(c)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.413#p-200.413(c))
- Older source: [RTC Appendix A, Prior Approval Matrix (November 12, 2020), 200.413(c), NSF column: Required](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/appendix-a-r.pdf)
- Older source: [2 CFR 200.413(c)(3) as in force from November 12, 2020 to September 30, 2024](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2020-11-12/title-2/part-200/section-200.413)
- Quote: "Direct charge the salaries of administrative and clerical staff if all conditions in 200.413 are met, excluding 200.413(c)(3)." (RTC Appendix A, row 200.413(c), where the RTC column says Waived and the NSF column says Required)
- Quote: "Such costs are explicitly included in the budget or have the prior written approval of the Federal awarding agency" (2 CFR 200.413(c)(3), edition in force on November 12, 2020)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-VISAS

Short-term visas are generally allowed and may be charged directly when they are critical and necessary for the project, allowable, consistent with the university's written policy, and meet the definition of a direct cost. Longer-term immigration visas are not covered by this allowance.

- Kind: allowed under a condition
- Source: [2 CFR 200.463(d)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.463#p-200.463(d))
- Quote: "Short-term visas (as opposed to longer-term immigration visas) are generally an allowable cost" (2 CFR 200.463(d))
- Checked: 2026-11-10

## Kinds of cost that are not allowed

Some of these have a narrow exception, written into the rule. Where the exception needs something "included in the award", the award notice or its approved budget has to show it.

### NSF-NEVER-ALCOHOL

Alcoholic beverages are not allowed.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.423](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.423)
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter II.D.2.f(xiii)(c)](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-2-proposal-preparation)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-PROMOTIONAL

Promotional items and memorabilia are not allowed, and neither is advertising or public relations meant only to promote the university. Gifts, t-shirts and memorabilia for participants are a separate case: they need NSF's prior written approval as participant support.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.421(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.421#p-200.421(e))
- Source: [NSF GC-1 (July 31, 2026), Term and Condition No. 3.a](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/gc1_7_31_2026.pdf)
- Older source: [NSF RTC Agency Specific Requirements (May 20, 2024), Article 13.a](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/files/nsf-research-terms-conditions-20240520-r.pdf)
- Older editions: the ASR edition of January 30, 2023 says the same in Article 13.a. The editions of November 12, 2020, October 4, 2021 and May 13, 2022 also require NSF's prior written approval, but send the request "via use of NSF's electronic systems" rather than through Research.gov.
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-ENTERTAINMENT

Entertainment, amusement, diversion and social activities, and costs tied to them such as tickets, meals, lodging, transportation and gifts, are not allowed. The only exception is a cost with a specific and direct programmatic purpose that is included in the award. Under the older 2 CFR wording, the exception covers a cost with a programmatic purpose that is authorized in the approved budget or has NSF's prior written approval.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.438(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.438#p-200.438(a))
- Source: [NSF PAPPG 24-1, Chapter II.D.2.f(xiii)(a)](https://www.nsf.gov/policies/pappg/24-1/ch-2-proposal-preparation)
- Older source: [2 CFR 200.438 as in force from November 12, 2020 to September 30, 2024](https://www.ecfr.gov/on/2024-09-30/title-2/part-200/section-200.438)
- Older answer: from the governing wording
- Quote: "Costs of entertainment, including amusement, diversion, and social activities and any associated costs are unallowable, except where specific costs that might otherwise be considered entertainment have a programmatic purpose and are authorized either in the approved budget for the Federal award or with prior written approval of the Federal awarding agency." (2 CFR 200.438, edition in force on September 30, 2024)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-DONATIONS

Contributions and donations from the university to other entities, in cash, property or services, are not allowed.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.434(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.434#p-200.434(a))
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-FINES

Fines, penalties, damages and settlements from the university's violation, alleged violation or failure to comply with laws and regulations are not allowed. The exceptions are costs that come from complying with specific provisions of the award, or that have NSF's prior written approval.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.441](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.441)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-FUNDRAISING

Organized fundraising, such as financial campaigns, endowment drives and soliciting gifts, is not allowed, and neither is investment counsel to increase investment income. Fundraising to meet the program's objectives is allowed only with NSF's prior written approval.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.442(a) and (b)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.442)
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-LOBBYING

Lobbying is not allowed: any attempt to influence elections or legislation, any attempt to improperly influence a federal award decision, and membership in organizations whose main purpose is lobbying. Technical and factual presentations on the award's topic made at a documented request of a legislature are one of the listed exceptions, but their travel, lodging and meals stay unallowable unless they are for testimony at a regularly scheduled Congressional hearing on the written request of the committee's Chairman or Ranking Minority Member.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.450(b), (c)(1) and (c)(2)(i)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.450)
- Source: [2 CFR 200.454(e)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.454#p-200.454(e))
- Checked: 2026-11-10

### NSF-NEVER-PERSONAL-USE

Goods or services for the personal use of the university's employees are not allowed, whether or not they are reported as taxable income. Housing and personal living expenses for employees are allowed only as direct costs approved in advance by NSF.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.445](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.445)

### NSF-NEVER-INTEREST

Interest on borrowed money, on temporary use of endowment funds, or on the university's own funds is not allowed. The exception is financing costs to buy, build or replace capital assets, under the conditions in 2 CFR 200.449.

- Older answer: from the governing wording

### NSF-NEVER-COVERED-DRONES

Taxes the university could have been exempted from are not allowed: an exemption available to it directly, or one based on the federal government's exemption when the federal agency makes the needed exemption certificates available. ### NSF-NEVER-AFFILIATED-RENTAL

### NSF-NEVER-OTHER-AWARD-LOSSES

Costs over income on any other award are not allowed, and neither are costs moved from another award because they exceeded that award's funding. A cost transfer whose only reason is that the other award ran out of money falls under this rule.

- Kind: not allowed
- Source: [2 CFR 200.451](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-2/part-200/section-200.451)

### NSF-PARTICIPANT-NOT-STAFF

Participant support costs are for participants, such as trainees, students or conference attendees, not for people who develop or deliver the award's activities: consultants, project personnel and the university's staff are not participants. Speakers and trainers generally aren't participants either, unless their main purpose at the event is to learn as a participant. The agent compares who was paid with the people named on the grant: a match is contradicts, citing both documents, and someone not named on the grant is supports. Under the older 2 CFR wording, which has no definition of Participant, participant support is for "participants or trainees (but not employees)" in connection with conferences or training projects, so it excludes only employees. On an award under the older wording, a named employee is still contradicts, and a named person who isn't an employee is unsure, with both readings in the reason. A speaker or trainer paid from participant support is contradicts unless their main purpose at the event was to learn as a participant, because every PAPPG edition in force for those awards (NSF 20-1, 22-1, 23-1 and 24-1) already said speakers and trainers generally aren't participants.

### NSF-LATE-EXTENSION

The university may extend the end date once, by up to 12 months, on its own, but it must notify NSF through Research.gov, with its reasons and the new period, at least ten calendar days before the end date. Extensions beyond that authority follow the procedures in PAPPG Chapter VI.D.3.c (GC-1 No. 6.c), which this rulebook hasn't read. So the agent flags a late notice, or any extension beyond that one-time authority (a second extension, or a first one longer than 12 months), unless NSF's approval of it, or NSF's own amendment notice for it, is on file. The one-time extension may not be used merely to spend the remaining balance, and an award with a zero balance can't be extended; the agent flags a notice that shows any of these.

### NSF-FRINGE-ON-STIPENDS

Fringe benefits follow the base that the university's own negotiated rate agreement names, so a fringe charge on a stipend is questionable when a rate term on the grant states the base as salaries and wages.
